Methodology & Sources

How the rankings were built, what the scores mean, and where the underlying coach data came from.

This analysis ranks 124 head coaches across the NFL, NBA, MLB and NHL using facial-proportion scores from the supplied Big 4 coaches workbook. Each coach has three separate image-level assessments, and the displayed coach score is the arithmetic mean of those three final image scores.

Explore the two score families below to see which facial distances each measure compares. The diagrams are illustrative, and all scores use a 10-point scale.

◆ Scoring explained

How the facial-proportion scores work

Each photograph is assessed using six relative facial measurements. The model compares these proportions with selected reference ratios to produce scores out of 10, grouped into two score families. Select a measure to see which distances it compares.

How the final score is calculated

The six underlying measurements contribute equally to each photograph’s final model score. The two family scores summarise those measurements; they are not counted again as extra inputs. A coach’s three final image scores are then averaged for the overall ranking.

The drawings illustrate the compared distances, not actual coach photographs or a reconstruction of the image-processing software. The source workbook provides calculated scores, not a step-by-step landmark-detection record.

Coaches ranked

124 head coaches across the NFL, NBA, MLB and NHL are included in the study.

Images analyzed

372 total image assessments were used, with three photographs evaluated for each coach.

Image-level score

The six individual measurements contribute equally to each photograph's final score. The two score-family composites summarise those measurements and are not counted again as separate inputs.

Final coach score

Each coach’s three final image scores are averaged to produce the displayed overall score. Rankings are ordered using unrounded averages, with displayed scores rounded to two decimal places.

How to interpret the scores

The scores reflect how closely each image aligns with the study’s facial-proportion model. They are not objective measures of attractiveness and do not measure coaching ability or professional performance.

Limitations

Results are photo-dependent and may be affected by factors such as lighting, camera angle, facial expression, image selection, cropping and other visual differences across photographs.

Important context: Attractiveness is subjective and can vary across individuals and cultures. This ranking reflects similarity to a selected set of facial proportions in the supplied images and should not be interpreted as a universal or definitive measure.
